# FARMERS IN ROMANIA — SLIDE BRIEFS

**Deck thesis:** Romania has a third of the EU's farms, and that headline is the least useful true thing
you can say about the country — because underneath it sit two entirely different agricultures, and the
official sources describing them disagree with each other by a factor of two.

14 slides. Shape: 1 title · 2–6 the structure, ending on the hinge · 7–10 money, output, volatility, trade ·
11–12 where the framing and the sources break down · 13–14 scope and close.

**EVERY number on a slide must end with its source and year, e.g. `Eurostat, 2023`. No exceptions.**
Anything on the DO-NOT-CLAIM list below may not appear at all.

## SLIDE 01 — Title
- Archetype: hero. Background glyph `fa-wheat-awn`.
- Eyebrow: `Romanian Agriculture &middot; By the Numbers, and Against Them`
- H1: **Farmers in Romania**
- Subtitle: One country, two agricultures, three official answers
- Pull-quote (italic): "Romania has more farms than any country in Europe. That is the least useful true thing you can say about it."
- Line under: Everything here is sourced to Eurostat, the European Commission or Romania's own statistics institute — and where they disagree, this deck says so.
- Stats strip (mono): `2.9M holdings &middot; 12.6M ha &middot; 90.2% under 5 ha &middot; Eurostat 2023`

## SLIDE 02 — The one number everyone quotes
- Eyebrow `The Headline` · H2 **A Third of Europe's Farms**
- Subtitle: Romania has 2.9 million agricultural holdings — 32.6% of the EU's 8.8 million. It is the most-quoted fact about Romanian agriculture and it explains almost nothing.
- Scale-drawn bar: Romania's 32.6% share of EU holdings against the rest of the EU-27, drawn to scale.
- Secondary stat row: `12.6 million ha utilised agricultural area = 8.0% of the EU total (Eurostat, 2023)`.
- **The puncture (glass-panel-warn):** a third of the farms working an eighth of the land. The number tells you nothing about who farms, how much they work, or whether they sell anything at all.
- Closer: "One in three European farms is Romanian. That is a fact about counting, not about farming."

## SLIDE 03 — Two agricultures, one country ⭐
- Eyebrow `The Structure` · H2 **Two Agricultures, One Country**
- Subtitle: The same 12.6 million hectares, split two ways that have almost nothing to do with each other.
- **The centrepiece: two drawn-to-scale bars on a shared 0–100% axis.**
  - Bar A — *farms*: 90.2% under 5 ha · 1.0% at 50 ha or more
  - Bar B — *land*: the under-5 ha group works roughly 23%; the 50 ha+ group works **53.1%**
  - (The ~23% is INS 2020: under 1 ha = 4.6% of UAA, 1–5 ha = 18.2%. Label it `INS, 2020` and say "roughly".)
- Two cards below: **The micro-holding** (2.6 million of them, mostly household plots, Eurostat 2023) vs **The estate** (1.0% of farms, 53.1% of the land, Eurostat 2023).
- Closer: "Nine in ten farms work a quarter of the land. One in a hundred works half of it. Any sentence beginning 'the Romanian farmer' is about one of these two and not the other."

## SLIDE 04 — The average farm is a lie
- Eyebrow `The Trap` · H2 **The Average Farm Got Bigger. Nothing Consolidated.**
- Subtitle: Romania's average holding rose from 3.45 ha to 4.42 ha between 2010 and 2020 — up 28%. It is a real number and it means the opposite of what it looks like.
- Left: the +28% rise, presented as good news would present it (`INS, 2010–2020`).
- Right, the puncture: Romania lost **1.4 million farms** between 2005 and 2023, a **33% decline — the largest of any EU country** (`Eurostat, 2005–2023`). The average rose because the bottom of the distribution was deleted, not because anyone merged fields.
- Small note: this is why a mean is the wrong statistic for a distribution shaped like Romania's.
- Closer: "The average went up because the smallest farms stopped existing. That is not consolidation. It is subtraction."

## SLIDE 05 — Who is actually farming
- Eyebrow `The People` · H2 **41.7% of Farm Managers Are Over 65**
- Subtitle: The structural question is not how many farms there are. It is who will be running them in ten years.
- Big stat: **41.7%** of Romanian farm managers are 65 or older (`Eurostat, 2023`).
- EU context row: across the EU, **10.7%** of farm managers are under 40 and **61.0%** are 55 or older (`Eurostat, 2023`).
- **Honest gap (glass-panel, inline `#8fa3b0` border):** Eurostat's page does not give Romania's own under-40 share, so this deck does not print one. Press estimates exist; none is primary-sourced.
- Closer: "Two in five Romanian farms are run by someone past retirement age. That is the succession question, and nobody has answered it."

## SLIDE 06 — THE HINGE: three official answers ⭐⭐
- Eyebrow `The Honest Slide` · H2 **How Many Romanians Work in Agriculture?**
- Subtitle: One question, one country, one year. Three official answers that differ by a factor of two.
- **Three cards, each with a green tick AND a `.stamp` reading `MEASURES SOMETHING ELSE`:**
  1. **20.7%** — Eurostat, national accounts, 2023. Highest share in the EU, against an EU average of 3.9%.
  2. **11.9%** — INS Labour Force Survey, 2023. Romania's own statistics institute, same year.
  3. **23%** — European Commission DG AGRI, CAP country page. **Undated**, so it cannot be pinned to a year at all.
- Below: they are not contradicting each other — they count different things (national accounts vs a household labour survey vs, probably, annual work units). But you cannot average them, and you cannot pick the one that suits your argument.
- Closer: "Before anyone argues about agricultural policy, they should have to say which of these three numbers they mean. Almost nobody does."

## SLIDE 07 — The money
- Eyebrow `The Money` · H2 **€15.83 Billion, and a Threshold**
- Subtitle: Romania's CAP Strategic Plan for 2023–2027, and the rule that decides who is in it.
- Breakdown bars: total **€15.83bn** = **€14.97bn** EU + **€0.86bn** national co-financing. Split: **€9.78bn** direct payments (Pillar I) · **€5.87bn** rural development (Pillar II) (`European Commission / EU CAP Network, 2023–2027`).
- Highlight: **€978.7 million** redistributed specifically to farms under 50 ha (`EC, 2023–2027`).
- The threshold card: to receive direct payments at all, a holding needs **€150 and/or 1 hectare** (`EC DG AGRI, 2023–2027`).
- **Caveat card (gold, must be labelled):** a per-hectare comparison circulates — roughly **€147/ha** for Romania against a **€410/ha** EU high — but the only source found for it is the **Thünen Institute, an academic secondary source**, not an EC or Eurostat page. Printed here with that caveat and no further weight.
- Closer: "Nearly a billion euro is aimed at small farms. The entry ticket is one hectare — which most of the two-point-six million do clear, and which tells you how low the bar has to be."

## SLIDE 08 — What the land produces
- Eyebrow `The Output` · H2 **Top Three in Europe for Grain**
- Subtitle: Whatever the structure looks like, the aggregate output is not marginal.
- Scale bars / stat cards: **3rd-largest maize producer** in the EU, 8.74 million tonnes (`Eurostat, 2023`) · **4th-largest wheat producer**, 9.60 million tonnes (`Eurostat, 2023`) · **largest sunflower area in the EU**, 1.08M ha in 2023 and 1.24M ha in 2024 (`Eurostat, 2023–2024`).
- Closer: "Romania is not a small agricultural country having a hard time. It is a large producer with a distribution problem."

## SLIDE 09 — The volatility ⭐
- Eyebrow `The Exposure` · H2 **A 63% Collapse in Six Years**
- Subtitle: Romanian maize yield, decade best against decade worst, drawn to scale.
- **Drawn-to-scale bars:** 2018 = **7.64 t/ha** (decade best) · 2024 = **2.83 t/ha** (decade worst). A **63% fall** (`Eurostat, apro_cpsh1, 2018 & 2024`).
- Context row: EU cereal output fell 5.1% in 2024; Romania's harvest fell **14.0%** and maize **31.7%** (`Eurostat, 2024`).
- Irrigation card: **443,870 ha irrigated = 3.5% of utilised agricultural area** in 2023 — up from **89,330 ha (0.6%)** in 2005 (`Eurostat Farm Structure Survey, 2005 & 2023`). Real improvement, still 3.5%.
- **Counter-evidence banner, said before anyone raises it:** 2024 was an explicit drought outlier. Using it as the representative year overstates the structural weakness. The point is the *range*, not the low.
- Closer: "The same fields produced 7.64 tonnes a hectare and 2.83 tonnes a hectare six years apart. That spread, not the average, is what a farmer actually plans against."

## SLIDE 10 — Raw out, processed in
- Eyebrow `The Paradox` · H2 **A Top-Three Grain Producer That Imports Its Food**
- Subtitle: The agri-food trade balance, and what it says about where value is added.
- Big movement: agri-food trade deficit **€3.2 billion in 2023 → €4.8 billion in 2024**, a **50% widening in one year** (`INS, reported via Agroberichten Buitenland, 2024`).
- Frame: commodity grain leaves, processed food arrives.
- **Counter-evidence (glass-panel-warn):** a widening food deficit is also what a catching-up economy looks like when household incomes rise and people buy more processed and premium food. It is evidence about value-added capacity; it is not on its own a verdict on the farm sector.
- **Sourcing note:** this figure is INS data reported through a secondary site — the weakest primary source in this deck, and labelled as such.
- Closer: "Selling wheat and buying bread is a policy outcome, not a fact of nature."

## SLIDE 11 — Where this framing is wrong
- Eyebrow `The Counter-Argument` · H2 **Four Things This Deck Is Probably Getting Wrong**
- Subtitle: Every slide so far has an argument built into it. Here is the case against each one, which an informed room would make anyway.
- Four cards:
  1. **"Small equals backward."** Many sub-5 ha holdings are semi-subsistence plots that feed rural households directly. Consolidation carries a real social cost — rural depopulation, lost livelihoods — that the efficiency framing elides.
  2. **"Romania is short-changed on CAP."** The low per-hectare rate comes out of convergence formulas fixed at 2007 accession, not a present-day decision. Romania is also shifting rural-development funds into direct payments and adding €978.7m of redistributive support.
  3. **"The trade deficit proves farming is failing."** It is equally consistent with rising incomes. See slide 10.
  4. **"2024 shows the real picture."** 2024 was a drought outlier. A multi-year average is less alarming and more honest.
- Closer: "If a deck only contains evidence that supports it, it is an argument wearing statistics. These four are the ones that would be thrown back at me, so they are on the slide."

## SLIDE 12 — When the sources disagree
- Eyebrow `The Fine Print` · H2 **Four Numbers With More Than One Official Value**
- Subtitle: Where the sources conflict, this is which one this deck used and why.
- `grid-cols-12` table — columns: **the question** (3) · **the competing values** (5) · **what this deck uses, and why** (4). Four rows:
  1. Share working in agriculture → 20.7% Eurostat / 11.9% INS / 23% DG AGRI undated → **all three, always with the methodology named** — the disagreement is the finding.
  2. Number of farms → 2.9M Eurostat 2023 / "almost 3.5 million" EU CAP Network, undated → **2.9M**, because it is dated and defined.
  3. Land worked by 50 ha+ farms → 53.1% Eurostat 2023 / 54.0% INS 2020 → **53.1%**, most recent and EU-comparable.
  4. Irrigation → 443,870 ha Eurostat 2023 / ~1.3M ha "prepared" Ministry 2022 / 2.15M ha FAO 2003–07 → **Eurostat's farm-survey figure**; the others measure network design capacity and a twenty-year-old survey.
- Closer: "Four of the numbers in this deck have a second official value. Picking one silently is how a presentation becomes propaganda."

## SLIDE 13 — What this deck does not cover
- Eyebrow `Scope, With Triggers` · H2 **What Is Not Here, and What Would Change That**
- Subtitle: Five deliberate omissions. None is a principle; each has a condition that would put it in.
- `grid-cols-12` trigger table — **omission** / **why** / **what would change it**:
  1. **No farm-level economics** — no sourced income, cost or margin data per holding was found at the resolution needed. *Changes when:* FADN farm-level data for Romania is pulled properly.
  2. **No regional breakdown** — national figures hide that the Bărăgan plain and the Apuseni mountains are not the same country. *Changes when:* someone needs a regional decision rather than a national picture.
  3. **No policy recommendation** — this deck describes a structure; it does not claim to know the fix. *Changes when:* there is a specific decision on the table.
  4. **No 2025–26 data** — provisional in-season estimates exist but are not in Eurostat's finalised dataset. *Changes when:* the 2025 harvest is finalised.
  5. **No non-EU comparison** — Ukraine, Serbia and Moldova would reframe the whole picture. *Changes when:* the question is competitiveness rather than structure.
- Closer: "None of these are principles. Each is a decision with a written trigger, so anyone can come back and pull one."

## SLIDE 14 — Close
- Eyebrow `What To Do With This` · H2 **Ask Which Number They Mean**
- Two columns.
- **Left — the three things worth carrying out of the room:**
  1. One in three EU farms is Romanian; nine in ten of them work a quarter of the land.
  2. The average farm grew because the smallest farms disappeared, not because anything consolidated.
  3. Two in five farm managers are over 65, and nobody has a sourced succession number.
- **Right — "What I still do not know"** (glass-panel, inline `#8fa3b0` border):

  - Whether the €147/ha figure survives contact with a primary EC source.
  - What share of the 2.6 million micro-holdings sell anything at all — the only figures found were from 2013 and a secondary site.
  - Whether any of this changes once 2025 and 2026 are finalised.
- **The ask (gold):** "Next time someone quotes a number about Romanian farming, ask them which of the three it is and what year it refers to. Most of the time there is no answer."

## DO NOT CLAIM — these may not appear on any slide

- "9.1 million EU farms / 31.8% Romania" (2020) — could not be confirmed on a live Eurostat page.
- Any Romania-specific **average farm size for 2023** — Eurostat gives only the EU-27 figure (17.8 ha). Never pair 17.8 ha with Romania's 4.42 ha (INS 2020) as if the same year or method.
- "Almost 3.5 million farmers" — undated, conflicts with Eurostat's dated 2.9 million.
- DG AGRI's 2016 Farm Structure Survey figures (3,422,030 holdings; 3.7 ha; 91.8% under 5 ha) unless explicitly labelled 2016 historical.
- The ARC2020 subsistence figures (~3M subsistence farms, 82% self-consumption, 25–30% of national food consumption) — secondary site **and** 2013 data.
- The Ministry/ANIF "1.3 million ha prepared, 38% irrigated" as a headline irrigation number.
- FAO AQUASTAT's 2.15 million ha "equipped for irrigation" — 2003–2007 survey data.
- Any Romania under-40 farm-manager percentage.
- 2025/2026 in-season crop estimates as confirmed figures.
- The Jan–Jul 2026 partial-year trade deficit alongside full-year figures.
- The €147/ha figure without naming the Thünen Institute and the word "secondary".
- DG AGRI's undated 23% and 44.3% presented as directly comparable to Eurostat's dated 20.7% and 41.7%.
